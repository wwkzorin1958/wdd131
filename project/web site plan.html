Site Plan · MacLehose Trail

A planning document for the MacLehose Trail guide website — a resource for hikers exploring Hong Kong's longest and most iconic long-distance trail.

1. Site Name

MacLehose Trail

maclehosetrail.org

The name directly references the trail itself — the MacLehose Trail — which is the central subject and purpose of the site. It is instantly recognizable to hikers and searchable for anyone planning a visit to Hong Kong.

Logo

The logo will be a simple wordmark using the site's primary font in deep forest green (#1e4d3a), optionally paired with a small mountain or trail marker icon (🏔️). The text "MacLehose Trail" is rendered in a clean, modern sans-serif that conveys clarity and outdoor reliability.

2. Site Purpose

The site provides a comprehensive, section-by-section guide to the MacLehose Trail — Hong Kong's 100 km long-distance hiking route. It offers distance, time, difficulty ratings, scenic highlights, and practical planning information (transport, water, sun protection). Visitors can track which sections they have completed, browse all ten sections at a glance, and find the essential details needed to plan a day hike or a full thru-hike.

The site's scope includes:

  • An overview of the entire trail with key statistics (100 km, 10 sections, 957 m highest point, established 1979).
  • Individual cards for each of the 10 sections with images, difficulty stars, distance, and time estimates.
  • An interactive progress tracker that saves completed sections in the browser.
  • A dedicated planning panel with practical advice (transport, water, sun protection, mobile coverage).
  • A references page citing all external images and information sources.

3. Scenarios

These are questions asked by the site's target audience — hikers, travelers, and outdoor enthusiasts planning to walk the MacLehose Trail.

“Which section of the MacLehose Trail is the most scenic, and how long does it take?”
The site answers this on the homepage: Section 2 (Long Ke → Pak Tam Au) is described as “Most scenic: Tai Long Wan, Sai Wan, Ham Tin beaches. ‘Hong Kong’s Maldives’,” with a distance of 13.5 km and an estimated time of 5–6 hours. The difficulty rating (★★★☆☆) helps the user gauge effort.
“What is the highest point on the trail, and which section reaches it?”
The hero stats grid shows “Highest point: 957 m,” and the Section 8 card explains: “Tai Mo Shan, highest peak in Hong Kong (957 m). Weather station, sea of clouds.” This directly connects the stat to the relevant section.
“How do I get to the trailheads, and what should I bring?”
The “Planning your hike” panel answers this with bullet points: “Buses & minibuses to trailheads,” “Carry 2–3 L water per person,” “Sun protection essential,” and “Mobile coverage varies.”
“Can I track which sections I have already completed?”
Yes — the interactive progress tracker on the homepage lets users mark sections as complete by clicking a card. The state is saved in localStorage and displayed as a progress bar (“Completed: X / 10 sections”).

7. Site Structure (Pages)

The site consists of at least three pages, all sharing consistent navigation and visual theme:

  1. index.html — Home page: hero, stats, progress tracker, all 10 section cards, planning panel.
  2. sections.html — Detailed list / filterable view of all sections (extends homepage content).
  3. plan.html — Planning guide: transport, water, sun, mobile coverage, best months, FAQ.
  4. references.html — Citations for all images, text, and external sources (linked in footer, unstyled as permitted).

Navigation links (Home · All sections · Plan · References) appear in the top bar of every page, and the references page is linked in the footer.

8. JavaScript Dynamic Elements

JavaScript for dynamic behavior:

  • Multiple functions: renderTrailCards(), updateProgressUI(), toggleSectionCompletion(), updateCardCompletionIndicators(), injectNavigation(), setupCardCompletionToggle().
  • DOM interaction: Selecting #trail-card-grid, modifying innerHTML, listening for click events on cards and the reset button.
  • Conditional branching: if (completed.includes(id)) … else … in the toggle function; if (completed) { … } else { … } in the card indicator function.
  • Objects & arrays: The trailSections array of objects; getCompletedSections() returns an array; Array.map(), Array.filter(), Array.includes().
  • Template literals: All dynamic HTML is built with backtick strings — e.g., the card markup, progress panel markup.
  • localStorage: Completed sections are saved under the key maclehose_completed_sections and persist across page reloads.
  • HTML form: A form is included on plan.html (e.g., “Ask a trail question” or “Newsletter sign-up”) meeting course standards.
